    Determination of hydrochloric acid by titration

    You have an unknown solution of hydrochloric acid, —, and a burette of sodium hydroxide standardised at 0.1000 mol/L. Find the molarity of the acid to three significant figures.

    The reaction

    HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H2O(l)

    One mole of acid per mole of base, so at the equivalence point

    cacid · Vacid = cbase · Vbase

    How you will know

    Phenolphthalein is colourless in acid and magenta above about pH 8.2. The endpoint is the first faint pink that survives swirling — not the deep pink that follows.

    Why three trials

    The first is a scouting run to find the endpoint roughly. The next two are the real measurements, delivered drop by drop, and they should agree within about 0.05 mL.

    The chemistry is not scripted

    The colour of the solution comes from the phenolphthalein equilibrium, which comes from the pH, which is solved exactly from a charge balance on the flask contents including water autoionisation. Nothing says “turn pink at 25 mL”.

    Errors that behave like real errors

    • An unconditioned burette holds a film of water that dilutes your titrant.
    • Air trapped below the stopcock escapes mid-run and moves the reading without titrant reaching the flask.
    • A water-wet pipette delivers 25.00 mL of slightly diluted sample.
    • Excess indicator consumes measurable titrant.
    • Your recorded readings are what your answer is built from — misread the meniscus and the error propagates, exactly as it would on the bench.

    Deliberately not automated

    Reading the meniscus, deciding the endpoint, and doing the arithmetic are yours. The digital readout in the instrument panel is a training aid and is reported as one.
