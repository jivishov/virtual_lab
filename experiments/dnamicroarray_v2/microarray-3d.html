    Finding your way

    The step card (top left) always shows what to do next. “Show me” highlights the target and brings it into view, and a soft ring marks the next target on the bench (switch it off in the Menu). Every panel can be dragged by its header, resized from its corners and collapsed; “Reset panel layout” in the Menu puts them back.

    Mouse, keyboard or touch

    Pick up the micropipette from the dock or its stand. Aim at a fresh tip, hold the left mouse button and move down about 28 pixels to fit it. To draw liquid, hold and lower into the source, then release. To deliver, hold and lower into the receiving well or to the card spot. Every stroke moves exactly 5 µL. Repeat a draw and a return three times to mix each sample.

    The camera frames each step automatically between strokes. Right-drag sideways to pan the bench and up or down to tilt the view; Shift + right-drag (or middle-drag) orbits all the way around; the wheel zooms toward the cursor. Auto-framing then waits until the next step, or until you choose “Auto” in the camera menu on the dock. For keyboard or touch operation, open “Targets” on the dock, choose a target and use it.

    On-device hand control

    Open “Hand control” on the dock and start the camera; the instruction under the video always says what to do now. Hold your hand as if gripping a micropipette, with the thumb on the plunger, and capture Rest (thumb relaxed) and Press (thumb pushed down) once. Your thumb then works the plunger as on a real pipette: a firm press counts even if it is not as deep as the calibration. Hold your hand where it feels comfortable. That spot becomes the centre of the bench (use “Set centre here” to change it), and small movements then cover the whole bench; “Cursor speed” sets how far. The pipette hovers straight above the cursor. Pause over a target until its ring turns gold and the cursor ring snaps onto it, then lower your hand to insert; spots you pass over on the way do not lock. Before inserting, slide sideways onto the next spot or lift to leave a lock; nothing is cancelled. To draw, press the plunger, lower the tip into the liquid, then release; to deliver, lower onto the target, then press. Pressing the plunger with an empty tip already in the liquid would blow bubbles, so it is not accepted and is noted in the debrief: keep it pressed, lift out, lower back in, then release. To mix, keep the tip in the well and press, then release. Lift your hand back up or move sideways to leave. Close your grip over a tool to pick it up. To put the micropipette away, bring it to its stand and show your open palm there; it hangs back in place. An open hand anywhere else only pauses handling. Aim assist enlarges targets and, in empty space near the next expected target, snaps to it; the spot under the cursor always wins. It never acts on its own and can be turned off. A thumb-to-index pinch plunger that needs no calibration is available under Hand settings. Tracking loss cancels an unfinished stroke and keeps the held tool and liquid.

    Saving and resuming

    Your run, notebook, answers and corrections are saved in this browser after every checked action. Reopening the page offers to resume, paused. Nothing is uploaded, and camera calibration is not stored. “Start a fresh experiment” in the Menu deletes the saved run.

    Procedure and model

    This version follows the supplied Edvotek #235 manual, version 235.180419: four patients, four controls and four genes on a 4 × 8 card. The A–D and E–H sets represent the same samples. Each reagent is a 200 µL aliquot in a snap-top microcentrifuge tube (1.5 mL assumed), identified by its cap colour and label. The QuickStrip wells hold prepared sample of unspecified volume; a small visual layer shows it, and only the added 5 µL control aliquots are counted.

    Liquids are shown near-clear, as buffers and cDNA appear in daylight. Red, green and yellow appear only as fluorescence under the long-wave UV lamp; the paper also glows blue, as in the manual's photograph (p.12). The Up control and red gene spots appear orange-red under UV.

    Every drying stage is five minutes at 37 °C or ten minutes at room temperature. Clock acceleration (Menu) is a teaching aid. Pause and Stop freeze elapsed time; Stop also cancels pending strokes. Hiding the tab pauses the clock.

    What the model represents

    Results reproduce the example on manual page 12. Yellow means similar expression in patient and control, red means greater expression in the patient, green means less, and dark means no detectable expression. Controls in columns 1–4 (Normal, Up, Down, Blank) must show their expected colours before genes are interpreted. It does not establish a diagnosis or identify a named gene.

    The scene uses approximate geometry and discrete laboratory actions. It does not simulate molecular kinetics, fluid dynamics, measured insertion distance, plunger force, partial dosing, photobleaching or calibrated fluorescence intensity. Real camera comfort and robustness need checking on classroom devices.
