AT THE BENCH

Controls & model boundaries

Direct mouse handling — no keyboard needed

Pick up: click a tool on the bench or in the dock. It follows your pointer without holding a button. Click the tool-return stand to park it. Label: select the fine-tip marker, then hold the left button and make a short writing stroke over each label pad. Each well is marked separately; a stationary click does not label it.

Fit a tip: aim at the tip rack, hold the left button and move the mouse down about 18 pixels. Release to lift. Aspirate: press and release the left button over the source. Dispense: press over the receiving well. A release after dispensing cannot accidentally aspirate. Mix: aspirate and dispense back into the same receiving standard, five complete cycles. Eject: right-click over tip waste with an empty tip.

Wash: pick up the transfer pipette, squeeze and release over the wash beaker to load, then squeeze over each well to deliver. Drain strips: click the tab at the left of a strip. Right-click to invert. Move it over paper towels, hold the left button and drag down to tap, then lift and repeat four times. Click its marked home position to return it. Repeat for the second strip. For final mixing, keep the strip upright and tap four times over the towels. Timer: click the bench timer. Right-drag orbits the camera; the wheel zooms.

Optional assistance

Guided assist and the keyboard/target selector are inside collapsed panels. They are not required for direct manipulation. Demonstrations and previews remain labeled as assisted or reference results, not independently completed work.

Hand gestures

Start the camera in this page’s own HTTPS tab and allow permission. Capture Rest and Press in the selected mode. Calibration is separate for free hand and a physical micropipette. The camera panel explicitly shows whether calibration is complete and whether the thumb press or rest is detected. Moving the cursor is not evidence of successful calibration. A preparation warning lists any unmarked wells; labeling must be finished before pipetting. Close your grip while over a tool to pick it up; open the hand for half a second to return it. A physical-pipette grip can also return a tool by lowering it onto the return stand.

To attach a tip, hold steady over the rack, then lower the palm about 4% of the camera image height. A thumb press cannot attach tips. To aspirate, hold over the source until the HUD says LOCKED, press the thumb, lower the palm until it says “Tip immersed”, then release the thumb. Once locked, vertical palm travel controls insertion depth only and cannot select a neighboring tube. Lift above the entry height or move sideways to withdraw and resume navigation. Lowering immediately after the press does not require another settling pause. If you withdraw before releasing, the aspiration is cancelled. Press the thumb over a receiving well to dispense. Repeat aspiration and dispensing for each mixing cycle. Over waste, the separately calibrated ejector works in real-pipette mode; an ordinary thumb press over waste can eject an empty tip in either mode. Rotate the wrist to invert a held strip and make four downward-and-upward tapping movements over the towels.

Camera inference uses the existing repository assets first, with a classic-worker implementation and a browser-thread fallback when workers or OffscreenCanvas are unavailable. Camera frames stay on the device. Loss of tracking cancels an unfinished stroke and requires a relaxed thumb before another press can register. The camera maps palm motion to virtual depth; it does not measure actual pressure, identify a physical pipette, or guarantee tracking under occlusion.

What is, and is not, simulated

This is a reference-informed, real-time 3D teaching model: geometric equipment, approximate physically based shading, screen-space glass, conserved liquid volumes and a constrained protocol. It is not photogrammetry, manufacturer CAD, a fluid simulation, a measured standard curve, or a validated diagnostic assay. The liquid-color response is illustrative, not measured from the kit photograph.

The kit says “fill” for washing but does not specify a numerical well fill volume or transfer-pipette capacity. This model uses 220 µL per well and a 1.32 mL transfer fill. Those are simulation choices, not kit specifications. The beaker starts at 55 mL following preparation p. 13; Appendix A instead lists approximately 45 mL. The 100 mL beaker is a scene design choice.

Antigen, primary and secondary incubations last 5 protocol minutes. TMB develops for 5 simulated minutes, within the specified 2–5 minute interval. Its development is synchronized after all wells are loaded for teaching clarity. Negative wells remain colorless. The stop step leaves this kit’s blue end-point visible.

The PDF supplied here does not provide patient contact histories. No histories or transmission chain are invented. Concentration alone is not used to prove who infected whom.

Running this page

The bench and protocol work without external JavaScript libraries. Camera tracking tries the repository’s existing vendor/mediapipe assets first on a hosted page, then pinned MediaPipe 0.10.17 CDN/model files. Camera permission, model-loading failures, and unavailable hardware are reported separately. A standalone local file needs internet access for the camera model, and the browser may require HTTPS or localhost. No analytics or new data collection are added by this independent file.
