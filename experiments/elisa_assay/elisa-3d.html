Controls & model limits

Mouse: no keyboard required

Click a tool on the bench or in the dock. The tool follows your pointer. To fit a tip, aim at the highlighted unused tip, hold the left button and move down about 28 pixels. Release to lift.

To aspirate, aim at the source, press and hold, move down until the contact panel shows immersion, then release. For delivery, press and lower into a receiving well; the fixed dose is dispensed once the tip is inside. Release does not aspirate back. To mix a standard, repeat actual draw-and-return cycles five times.

Use the marker to make a short stroke across each label pad. Numbers are generated labels; this is not handwriting recognition. The drawn stroke is retained on the pad.

Right-click over waste to eject an empty tip. Left-click over waste to discard the final dilution aliquot. For washing, use the transfer pipette and beaker. Pick up each strip, right-click to invert, then lower and lift over towels four times. Keep strips upright for final mixing. Return each strip to its home with the Return button or its home target.

Observer camera: right-drag to orbit around the bench. Hold Shift while right-dragging for a mild horizon tilt (limited to ±7°), and use the mouse wheel to zoom. The Bench, Strips, Reagents, and Overhead buttons return to stable presets. Camera changes cancel pending liquid-transfer strokes. “Locate next target” only highlights the next target; it does not perform an action.

Webcam gestures

Camera panel: drag its header to move it anywhere on the screen; drag the lower-right corner to resize it. Sensitivity controls how far virtual tools move for a given hand movement. Reactivity controls smoothing: lower values are steadier but respond more slowly.

Start the camera and capture your relaxed and pressed thumb positions while holding a comfortable pipette-like grip. Close your grip over a tool briefly to pick it up. Once held, the grip is latched independently of the thumb.

Hold over a vessel until its opening locks. Press the thumb, lower the palm until the tip is immersed, and release to aspirate. Lower into a receiving well and press to deliver. Lift above the entry position or move sideways to leave the locked opening. Tip fitting uses palm lowering, not a thumb press. Turn the palm over to invert a held strip. For the marker, open the hand and deliberately turn the palm downward for about half a second to place it on the bench; simply opening the hand is not enough. Other tools can still be returned by holding an open hand for about one second outside a locked opening.

Camera loss cancels pending actions and retains the held tool. It never counts as releasing the plunger. Relax the thumb after tracking resumes. The selected hand is kept stable; this is not simultaneous two-handed manipulation.

What this version does and does not model

The existing protocol engine retains its 50 µL micropipette dose, 220 µL wash fill and 1.32 mL model transfer-pipette capacity. Thumb travel animates the plunger but does not measure force or continuously meter volume. Partial doses, second-stop blowout, seal mechanics, fluid dynamics and calibrated optical absorbance are not simulated. These limitations are not hidden behind a physics engine.

Geometry and materials are reference-informed approximations, not measured CAD or photogrammetry. Palm movement maps to virtual insertion depth, not measured distance. Alignment assistance is used near openings. The blue kit-specific endpoint and dilution rules are preserved. Clear liquids receive a faint cool tint and stronger surface contrast only so students can distinguish liquid level from transparent plastic; this tint is a visual aid, not a claim about reagent color. This page uses the existing WebGL2 renderer; Babylon.js/Havok and hardware-specific interfaces are not included.

Real webcam robustness and user comfort still require testing on the school devices. The page uses local MediaPipe assets first, with pinned CDN fallbacks. Browser/GPU/network compatibility is not universal.
