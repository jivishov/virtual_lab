      At the ELISA bench

      Finding your way

      The step card (top left) always shows what to do next. “Show me” highlights the target and brings it into view, and a soft ring marks the next target on the bench (switch it off in the Menu). Every panel can be dragged by its header, resized from its corners and collapsed; “Reset panel layout” in the Menu puts them back.

      Mouse, keyboard or touch

      Pick up a tool from the bench or the dock. To fit a tip, aim at the marked unused tip, hold the left mouse button and move down about 28 pixels. To draw, hold and lower into the source until the contact panel shows immersion, then release. To deliver, hold and lower into the receiving well; the fixed 50 µL dose is dispensed once the tip is in. Mix a serial-dilution well with five draw-and-return cycles. Sweep the marker across each well’s label pad. Right-click over the waste to eject an empty tip; left-click there to discard the final aliquot. Right-click without dragging inverts a held strip; then lower and lift it over the towels four times. Click a tool’s stand (or use Return tool) to put it away.

      The camera frames each step automatically between strokes. Right-drag sideways to pan the bench and up or down to tilt the view; Shift + right-drag (or middle-drag) orbits all the way around; the wheel zooms toward the cursor. Auto-framing then waits until the next step, or until you choose “Auto” in the camera menu on the dock. For keyboard or touch operation, open “Targets” on the dock, choose a target and use it.

      On-device hand control

      Open “Hand control” on the dock and start the camera; the instruction under the video always says what to do now. Hold your hand as if gripping a micropipette, with the thumb on the plunger, and capture Rest (thumb relaxed) and Press (thumb pushed down) once. Your thumb then works the plunger (or the bulb of the transfer pipette) as on the real tool: a firm press counts even if it is not as deep as the calibration. Hold your hand where it feels comfortable; that spot becomes the centre of the bench (“Set centre here” changes it), and “Cursor speed” sets how far small movements reach. The tool hovers straight above the cursor. Pause over a target until its ring turns gold and the cursor ring snaps onto it, then lower your hand to insert; wells you pass over on the way do not lock. Before inserting, slide sideways onto the next well or lift to leave a lock; nothing is cancelled.

      To draw, press the plunger, lower the tip into the liquid, then release; to deliver, lower into the well, then press. Pressing with an empty tip already in the liquid would blow bubbles, so it is not accepted: keep it pressed, lift out, lower back in, then release. To mix a serial-dilution well, keep the tip in the well and press, then release. Close your grip over a tool to pick it up. Label a well as with a real marker: pause over its label pad until the ring turns gold, lower your hand until the nib touches, then make a short sideways stroke; pads you pass over are never written on. Turn your palm over to invert a held strip, pause over the towels and lower and lift four times. To put a tool away, bring it to its stand (the pipette stand, the transfer-pipette holder, the marker tray or the strip’s place on the sheet) and show your open palm there; the marker may also be laid down by turning the open palm downward. An open hand anywhere else only pauses handling. Aim assist enlarges targets and, in empty space near the next expected target, snaps to it; it never acts on its own. A thumb-to-index pinch plunger that needs no calibration is available under Hand settings. Tracking loss cancels an unfinished stroke and keeps the held tool and liquid.

      What this version does and does not model

      The protocol engine keeps its 50 µL micropipette dose, 220 µL wash fill and 1.32 mL model transfer-pipette capacity. Thumb travel animates the plunger but does not measure force or continuously meter volume. Partial doses, second-stop blowout, seal mechanics, fluid dynamics and calibrated optical absorbance are not simulated. Clear liquids receive a faint cool tint only so students can distinguish the liquid level from transparent plastic; the blue kit-specific end point and dilution rules are preserved. Geometry and materials are reference-informed approximations, not measured CAD. Palm movement maps to virtual insertion depth, not measured distance. Pause and Stop freeze the incubation clock; Stop also cancels pending strokes. Real webcam comfort and robustness need checking on the school devices.
