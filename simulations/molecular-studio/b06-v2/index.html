Field notes
Model and credits

Each hub has exactly the electron-domain sockets required by its Lewis structure: two through six, with no extra holes. One socket represents a bond direction or a lone-pair region, not an individual valence electron. Single, double and triple bonds use one socket each, but contribute one, two and three shared electron pairs. Carbon has total bond order four and no lone pair in these cases. The socket directions give ideal VSEPR angles. Sphere sizes, pair markers and arrow lengths are classroom conventions. Experimental angles and dipole magnitudes can differ.

Connector lengths share one scale and stay fixed while you turn or move a piece. The Bond workshop identifies representative bond distances or estimates from covalent radii. Estimates use the two radii added together, multiplied by 0.88 for a double bond or 0.78 for a triple bond. These factors are classroom approximations. PCl₅, SF₄, SeF₄ and BrF₅ supply distinct axial and equatorial lengths. Other equivalent links share one length; values are not a force-field calculation.

Single bonds permit rotation of attached groups; a double bond maintains its relative orientation and triple-bond ends are linear. Ethane’s CH₃ group can turn without stretching its bonds. Torsional energy barriers, collisions, vibration, resonance and reactions are not simulated. Branch groups arrive preassembled; the exercise counts domains at the labelled central hub.

The central amber inserts each represent one lone pair. Paired dots on an end piece count its terminal lone pairs; they do not affect the central domain count. Five-domain lone pairs occupy equatorial sites. Two lone pairs in a six-domain model sit opposite one another.
