Room 0

The Temperature Bridge

Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics
If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
Think of it this way: A thermometer reads 37 °C when touching your forehead, and also 37 °C when touching a warm cup of soup. Without touching each other, you and the soup must be at the same temperature. That's the zeroth law in action.

The Energy Vault

First Law of Thermodynamics
Energy cannot be created or destroyed — only transferred or converted from one form to another. The change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done: ΔU = Q − W.
Think of it this way: When you pump up a bicycle tire, the pump gets warm. The work you do (pushing the pump) converts into heat, raising the air's internal energy. No energy appeared from nowhere — it all came from your muscles.

Room 2 — The Entropy Maze

Second Law of Thermodynamics

Room 2

The Entropy Maze

Second Law of Thermodynamics
In any spontaneous process, the total entropy of an isolated system always increases. Heat flows naturally from hot to cold, never the reverse, without external work.
Think of it this way: An ice cube melts in a warm room — heat flows from the warm air into the cold ice. You never see a puddle of water spontaneously freeze while warming the room around it. That one-way direction is the second law.

Third Law of Thermodynamics

Room 3

Absolute Zero

Third Law of Thermodynamics
As the temperature of a perfect crystal approaches absolute zero (0 K, or −273.15 °C), its entropy approaches zero. It is impossible to reach absolute zero in a finite number of steps.
Think of it this way: At absolute zero, particles would be in perfect order with no random motion at all — zero disorder means zero entropy. But every time you try to cool something further, it gets harder and harder, like a curve that gets closer and closer to zero but never quite touches it.
